## Combat rule to carry into Wishfire

Early ordinary combat should keep the starter Wishfire Heroes alive without requiring Kaja; zero starter Hero KOs is the owner's gauntlet requirement. StoneAge kept sampled trainers standing behind pets, and its Chapter 1-10 report showed an apparent pet KO, so trainer survival is a qualitative reference rather than proof that every front-line Wishfire Hero should behave like a trainer. A chapter boss can still stop progress by exhausting its gauge. In a simplified encounter model, ordinary incoming burst must stay below each exposed Hero's current HP, and damage accumulated between recovery opportunities must remain within the party's sustain budget. For an underprepared boss, `party DPS × active boss time` falls short of boss effective HP while the party survives that interval. Earned progression should raise damage enough to cross the boss clear threshold. These are design inequalities inferred from outcomes; they are not StoneAge's hidden formula.

Wishfire's current candidate uses its own rules: `max(0, attack power − resistance)` before percentage modifiers in `calculateDamageRules.mjs`; fodder and routine single hits are capped at 2% of target max HP in `routineEnemyScaling.mjs`; living Heroes regain 15% of max HP after an ordinary wave in `continuousCombatStage.mjs`; bosses use the authored 18-second gauge. The 2% cap and 15% respite are provisional Wishfire interventions chosen to address measured early attrition, not values derived from StoneAge. The 18-second timer is a Wishfire player-guide contract, not a measured StoneAge timer. At starter HP the capped positive hit is often one point; neither current report records pre-cap damage nor the fraction of hits that were capped. Retain current HeroProgress identities and active instance-key equipment when evaluating it. Saved canonical gear keys previously failed to equip deployed clones, so older saved-loadout reports cannot demonstrate gear protection.
